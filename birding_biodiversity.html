Trees and Ecosystem • Bioblitz

🎯 Objective

Students will observe a tree as a living ecosystem, identifying signs of plant growth, insect activity, and bird life and understanding a tree’s role as primary producer.

📖 Overview

Observing nature with curiosity can help students cultivate a sense of stewardship for the land and its ecosystems. As they track their observations, students will practice key skills such as note-taking, research, drawing, and inquiry.

📦 Materials

  • Clipboards
  • Birding and Biodiversity tracking chart
  • Pencils, colored pencils, or crayons
  • Optional: Magnifying glasses
  • Optional: Binoculars

🔬 Method

Introduction

Schoolyard Tree Habitat Discussion

Visit a tree on your schoolyard campus. (Ideally — one of the trees that your class has "adopted" as a part of the Tree Stewardship lesson).

Class discussion: What do you think lives in this tree? Connect to vocabulary: habitat, primary producer, observation.

Remind students that trees produce their own food from sunlight and that many birds, animals, and insects rely on trees for food and shelter.

Observation

Field Scouting Checklist

🐛 Insect Check

How many insects can you find on your tree?

If possible: Take a photo of the insect and identify using iNaturalist.

🐦 Bird Check

Do you see any birds on your tree or nearby? They might be perching, singing, eating, or nesting in your tree.

Identify the species and count how many. If possible: Use Merlin Bird ID to identify using sound.

🦎 Animal Check

Have you seen any animals (lizards, squirrels, butterflies) near your tree?

If possible: Identify using iNaturalist.

Recording

Nature Journaling & Dashboard Entry

Create a nature journal with a page dedicated to the nature that was observed today. Have students use the Nature Journaling video as an example.

Ask students to draw the birds or insects that they have observed, paying special attention to their size, shape, and color patterns.

Once back inside, record all observations on the class chart and into the UTRGV dashboard.

💡

Extension & Habitat Enhancement

Enhancing your school’s habitat for birds with proper nesting sites (like the installed birdhouses or nest boxes), food and water resources will promote bird activity and increase opportunities for observation. Learning more about the birds you may encounter in hands-on activities will help foster a greater appreciation for them and can promote better engagement while observing habitats.

TIP: The Merlin Bird ID app is a fun, free resource that can be helpful in identifying birds! It features photos, bird calls, and general information about the bird.

🤠 SOUTH TEXAS BIRDING METAPHOR Pedagogical Visual Analogy

"Parts of a Cowboy" Bird Anatomy Explorer

Comparing bird anatomical field marks to parts of a South Texas cowboy outfit for memorable elementary inquiry!

🤠
The Cowboy Hat
➔ Crown & Crest

Like a cowboy tipping his Stetson, birds raise their crown feathers to show excitement, make calls, or spot friends!

Example: Great Kiskadee, Northern Cardinal
🧣
The Silk Bandana
➔ Throat Patch & Gorget

A bright neckerchief around the throat that shines in sunlight to identify species and signal other scouts.

Example: Ruby-throated & Buff-bellied Hummingbird
🪢
The Rope / Lasso
➔ Beak Adaptation & Tool

A specialized ranch tool! Hooked for cracking thick mesquite seed pods, pointed for probing nectar, or chisel-sharp for wood boring.

Example: Chachalaca (curved), Woodpecker (chisel)
👢
Boots & Spurs
➔ Talons & Perching Feet

Sturdy gripping gear built for walking through thorny brush or clamping tightly to high canopy twigs during Rio Grande winds.

Example: Green Jay (perching), Harris's Hawk (talons)

Birding by Ear & Visual Field ID

Click the sound button to practice identifying signature Rio Grande Valley birds by ear before heading outside!

Plain Chachalaca

Ortalis vetula

RGV Star: Loud raucous chorus early in the morning! Love eating berries from native Anacua and Sugar Hackberry trees.

🟡

Great Kiskadee

Pitangus sulphuratus

Bandit Mask: Bright yellow chest with black and white head stripes. Shouts its own name: "Kis-ka-DEE!"

🦜

Green Jay

Cyanocorax yncas

Canopy Gem: Vibrant green body, bright blue crown, and yellow outer tail feathers. Very smart acorn cachers!

🪵

Golden-fronted Woodpecker

Melanerpes aurifrons

Trunk Inspector: Zebra-striped back with golden yellow nape. Pecks tree bark to find hidden beetles and caterpillars!
