## 1. Executive Statement of Attribution & Lineage

The **Comprehensive TEKS Forest Literacy Learning Architecture** is built upon decades of foundational research, pedagogical frameworks, and ecological studies conducted by state forestry agencies, university agroecology laboratories, indigenous scholars, and conservation education pioneers. 

In adherence to project governance and academic integrity standards, this document establishes the formal provenance and attribution for all pedagogical constructs, ecological formulas, and cultural frameworks incorporated into our knowledge graph and lesson modules.

## 2. Core Organizational & Pedagogical Frameworks

### 2.1. Project Learning Tree (PLT) & Sustainable Forestry Initiative (SFI)
* **Foundational Framework:** *Forest Literacy Framework: Translating the Forest to K-12 Classrooms* (Project Learning Tree, 2020).
* **Core Conceptual Structure:** The four-theme pedagogical inquiry model:
  1. *Theme 1: What is a forest?* (Biological and ecological foundations).
  2. *Theme 2: Why do forests matter?* (Ecosystem services, cultural connection, economics).
  3. *Theme 3: How do we sustain our forests?* (Adaptive management, urban forestry, silviculture).
  4. *Theme 4: What is our responsibility to forests?* (Stewardship, civic action, personal ethics).
* **Attribution Note:** Concepts in our knowledge graph are indexed to PLT Forest Literacy Themes 1–4, establishing national cross-compatibility for educators transitioning between state TEKS and national forestry curricula.
* **Citation:** Project Learning Tree. (2020). *Forest Literacy Framework*. Sustainable Forestry Initiative, Washington, D.C. Available: [https://www.plt.org/forest-literacy-framework](https://www.plt.org/forest-literacy-framework).

### 2.4. USDA Forest Service & Davey Tree Expert Co. (i-Tree Ecosystem Suite)
* **Scientific Foundations:**
  * *i-Tree Eco* Peer-Reviewed Urban Forestry Modeling Algorithms.
  * *Natural Inquirer* K-12 Scientific Journal (USDA Forest Service & Cradle of Forestry in America Interpretive Association).
* **Contribution to Modules:**
  * Equations for calculating:
    * Annual carbon sequestration (\(\text{kg } \text{CO}_2 / \text{yr}\)) based on DBH (Diameter at Breast Height) and growth rates.
    * Stormwater interception and retention (\(\text{gallons } \text{H}_2\text{O} / \text{yr}\)).
    * Air pollutant particulate removal (\(\text{PM}_{2.5}, \text{O}_3, \text{NO}_2, \text{SO}_2\)).
    * Building energy cooling savings (\(\text{kWh / year}\)).
* **Citation:** Nowak, D. J., Crane, D. E., & Stevens, J. C. (2006). *Air pollution removal by urban trees and shrubs in the United States*. Urban Forestry & Urban Greening, 4(3-4), 115-123.  
* **Citation:** USDA Forest Service. (2022). *i-Tree Suite v6.x: Peer-Reviewed Urban Forest Assessment Tools*. Davey Institute & USFS Northern Research Station.

## 3. Indigenous Ethnobotany & Cultural Lineage of South Texas (RGV)

### 3.1. Tamaulipan Biome & Coahuiltecan Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK)
* **Scholarly Foundations:**
  * Ethnobotanical documentation of the Coahuiltecan, Karankawa, and Lipan Apache nations across the Rio Grande delta and Northern Tamaulipas.
  * Traditional uses of the *Pechita* (Mesquite bean pod), *Nopal* (Prickly pear cactus), and *Anacahuita* (Mexican olive).
* **Contribution to Modules:**
  * *Pechita* milling: High-protein, low-glycemic flour processing and seasonal harvesting cycles.
  * Natural dyes and medicinal teas derived from Honey Mesquite gum, Retama bark, and Texas Ebony seeds.
  * Ecological philosophy of reciprocal kinship (*All My Relations*) and sustainable pruning that stimulates canopy health rather than clear-cutting.
* **Citations:**
  * Foster, W. C. (2008). *Historic Native Peoples of the Texas Coast and Lower Rio Grande*. University of Texas Press.
  * Tull, D. (2013). *Edible and Useful Plants of Texas and the Southwest: A Practical Guide*. University of Texas Press.
  * Cavazos, R. (2016). *Native Flora and Cultural Traditions of the Rio Grande Valley*. Edinburg, TX: UTRGV Regional History Archives.

## 4. Modern Environmental Epidemiology & Microclimate Science

### 4.1. Low-Cost Particulate Matter (\(\text{PM}_{2.5}\)) & Urban Heat Island (UHI) Research
* **Scientific Foundations:**
  * EPA and World Health Organization (WHO) air quality monitoring criteria.
  * Recent advances in low-cost optical particle counters (e.g., Plantower PMS5003 / PurpleAir networks) and their role in identifying localized schoolyard pollution hot spots.
  * Pediatric vulnerability to extreme heat: Thermoregulatory physiology in developing children, heat exhaustion mechanisms, and asthma exacerbation dynamics in Hidalgo County.
* **Contribution to Modules:**
  * Higher education lab protocols for sensor calibration against Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) reference continuous air monitoring stations (CAMS).
  * High school / middle school environmental justice modules examining historical redlining, canopy deficit, and surface heat correlations.
* **Citations:**
  * Morawska, L., et al. (2018). *Applications of low-cost sensing technologies for air quality monitoring and exposure assessment*. Atmospheric Environment, 186, 321-355.
  * UT Southwestern Medical Center & Texas DSHS. (2023). *Pediatric Heat-Related Illness and Emergency Department Encounters in South Texas*. Texas Public Health Journal.
  * Harlan, S. L., et al. (2006). *Neighborhood microclimates and vulnerability to heat stress*. Social Science & Medicine, 63(11), 2847-2863.

## 5. Contemplative Ecology & *Shinrin-yoku* (Forest Bathing)

### 5.1. Psychophysiological Benefits of Canopy Immersion
* **Scientific Foundations:**
  * Japanese Forestry Agency research on *Shinrin-yoku* (forest bathing) and phytoncide emissions (antimicrobial volatile organic compounds released by trees).
  * Attention Restoration Theory (ART) and stress recovery theory in educational settings.
* **Contribution to Modules:**
  * Campus quiet contemplation and sensory outdoor meditation protocols.
  * Nature journaling and mindfulness exercises designed to reduce academic stress and cognitive fatigue.
* **Citations:**
  * Miyazaki, Y. (2018). *Shinrin-yoku: The Japanese Way of Forest Bathing for Health and Relaxation*. Timber Press.
  * Kaplan, R., & Kaplan, S. (1989). *The Experience of Nature: A Psychological Perspective*. Cambridge University Press.
  * Li, Q. (2010). *Effect of forest bathing trips on human immune function*. Environmental Health and Preventive Medicine, 15(1), 9-17.
