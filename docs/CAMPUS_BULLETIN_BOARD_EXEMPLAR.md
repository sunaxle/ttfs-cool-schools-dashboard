## 1. Executive Summary

This catalog documents the **20 representative student submissions** pinned to the physical campus bulletin boards across Months 1, 2, and 3. These submissions demonstrate how elementary and middle school students actively engage in tree stewardship, outdoor TEKS science investigations, creative writing, and biodiversity spotting.

Each monthly collective submission is verified by campus leads and unlocks official digital **Stewardship Badges** on the regional dashboard.

## 3. Detailed Artifact Breakdown by Category

### Category A: Tree Adoptions & Stewardship Pledges
1. **Item 01 — Tree Adoption Tag #104: "El Gigante Verde" (M. Rivas Primary):**  
   *Artifact Description:* Adoption certificate pinned with green yarn to a sapling polaroid. Students pledged to inspect weekly soil moisture and record monthly growth.
2. **Item 10 — Tree Adoption Tag #78: "The Elm Street Guardians" (J.W. Caceres Intermediate):**  
   *Artifact Description:* 5th-grade student council adoption card for the Cedar Elm in the bus drop-off loop, committing to mulch protection and litter sweeps.
3. **Item 18 — Tree Adoption Tag #212: "Baby Oak Charlie" (Mercedes Academic Academy):**  
   *Artifact Description:* High school environmental leaders partnered with kindergarten buddies to adopt a Mexican White Oak, installing an irrigation drip bag.

---

### Category B: Student Poetry & Micro-Stories
4. **Item 02 — Student Poem: "Underneath the Shade" (M. Rivas Primary):**  
   *Student Verse:*  
   > *"The Texas sun is big and bright, it bakes the playground blacktop tight.*  
   > *But under our Cedar Elm so tall, we feel no burning heat at all.*  
   > *Cool green leaves whisper in the breeze, thank you, thank you, for our trees!"*
5. **Item 08 — Bilingual Haiku: "Afternoon Mesquite Breeze" (Ruben Hinojosa Elementary):**  
   *Student Verse:*  
   > *"Hojas en el sol (Leaves in the sun),*  
   > *Brisa fresca en la tarde (Cool breeze in the afternoon),*  
   > *Sombra para todos (Shade for everyone)."*
6. **Item 13 — Student Micro-Story: "The Day the Cicadas Sang" (Singleterry Elementary):**  
   *Reflection:* During outdoor reading hour, 4th graders documented a choir of cicadas singing in Tree #19's canopy, making the outdoor space feel vibrant and alive.

---

### Category C: Nature Art, Bark Studies & Leaf Rubbings
7. **Item 03 — Wax Leaf Rubbing: Southern Live Oak (J.W. Caceres Intermediate):**  
   *Artifact Description:* Crayon leaf rubbing comparing the waxy, thick cuticle of drought-hardy native oak leaves against non-native garden plants.
8. **Item 06 — Colored Pencil Sketch: "Texas Ebony in Bloom" (Sgt. Manuel Chacon Middle):**  
   *Artifact Description:* Botanical sketch illustrating the zigzag spiny twigs, fragrant cream flowers, and dark seed pods of *Ebenopsis ebano*.
9. **Item 12 — Soil Cross-Section: "Roots Underground Network" (Capt. D. Salinas II STEAM):**  
   *Artifact Description:* Science diagram showing how taproots anchor trees against hurricane-force winds and filter 450 gallons of stormwater runoff.
10. **Item 15 — Split Art Comparison: "Playground Heat vs. Shade Oasis" (C. Stainke Intermediate):**  
    *Artifact Description:* Left side illustrates an unshaded 140°F metal playground slide; right side shows children reading comfortably under a tree canopy.
11. **Item 17 — Charcoal Bark Texture Study: Retama Photosynthesis (A.P. Solis Middle):**  
    *Artifact Description:* Microscopic bark rubbing highlighting green chlorophyll in Retama bark that allows it to produce energy even during dry periods without leaves.
12. **Item 20 — All-School Vision Mural: "Our School Forest in 2040" (M. Rivas Primary):**  
    *Artifact Description:* Collective time-lapse mural illustrating the projected 30% mature tree canopy when today's elementary students graduate from college.

---

### Category D: Wildlife Spotting & Habitat Observations
13. **Item 04 — Monarch Butterfly Migration Log (Capt. D. Salinas II STEAM):**  
    *Observation:* Kindergarteners recorded a Monarch (*Danaus plexippus*) feeding on native milkweed planted alongside new saplings.
14. **Item 07 — Green Anole Camouflage Observation (W.B. Travis Elementary):**  
    *Observation:* 4th graders documented a green anole changing color from bright green to bark-brown while hunting ants on Anacua Tree #55.
15. **Item 11 — Green Jay in the Courtyard (Patricia S. Garza Elementary):**  
    *Observation:* 3rd graders identified the iconic South Texas Green Jay (*Cyanocorax yncas*) feeding on orange Anacua berries.
16. **Item 16 — Texas Spiny Lizard on Oak Bark (A.M. Ochoa Primary):**  
    *Observation:* 2nd graders noted how the spiny lizard's rough scales matched the fissured bark of mature Live Oaks.

---

### Category E: Heat Science, Math & Hands-On Care
17. **Item 05 — Watering Squad: "Cracker Dirt Test" (M. Rivas Primary):**  
    *Log Entry:* Summer stewardship squad checked soil 2 inches deep, noted dry cracker-like soil, and delivered 5 gallons of harvested rainwater.
18. **Item 09 — Infrared Surface Heat Hunt: 51.3°F Delta (M. Rivas Primary):**  
    *Data Log:* Unshaded blacktop measured **138.4°F** vs. **87.1°F** under the Live Oak canopy (a 51.3°F cooling benefit during peak afternoon sun).
19. **Item 14 — Tree Caliper & Trunk Expansion Chart (Sgt. William G. Harrell Elementary):**  
    *Data Log:* 5th-grade math team recorded trunk DBH growing from **3.2 inches in June to 3.8 inches in August** (+0.6" growth in 60 days).
20. **Item 19 — Rainwater Cistern Flow Log: 350 Gallons Harvested (W.B. Travis Elementary):**  
    *Log Entry:* Green club tracked roof runoff after an August tropical storm, harvesting 350 gallons to water 24 newly planted trees.
