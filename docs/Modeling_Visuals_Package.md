## 1. Overview & Purpose

This document provides standardized high-resolution environmental modeling figures, projection placeholders, and caption conventions for integration into grant reporting, board presentations, and school district briefings.

The visual assets model two core phenomena across the 14 participating elementary school campuses in Donna ISD and Mercedes ISD:
1. **Diurnal Surface Heat & Microclimate Dynamics:** Baseline morning, midday, and evening thermal surface conditions.
2. **Longitudinal Tree Canopy Projections:** Estimated 5-year and 10-year shade expansion and ambient temperature reduction (°F) resulting from Cool Schools tree plantings.

#### Figure Captions & Metadata

* **Figure 1.1A (Morning Baseline - 9:00 AM):**  
  *Image Source:* `docs/heatmap_morning.png`  
  *Caption:* High-resolution thermal simulation of morning campus conditions. Unshaded hardscape demonstrates rapid heat absorption (102°F) relative to natural ground cover (91°F) and early canopy shade (81°F).

* **Figure 1.1B (Midday Peak - 1:00 PM):**  
  *Image Source:* `docs/heatmap_midday.png`  
  *Caption:* Peak solar irradiance modeling during recess and outdoor physical activity periods. Surface temperatures on unshaded blacktop reach extreme thresholds (138°F), while mature canopy buffers reduce surface temperatures to 87°F (a 51°F differential).

* **Figure 1.1C (Evening Retention - 5:00 PM):**  
  *Image Source:* `docs/heatmap_evening.png`  
  *Caption:* Thermal mass retention during after-school dismissal. Unshaded artificial surfaces radiate stored heat (126°F), intensifying local heat island effects, whereas vegetative buffers cool rapidly (85°F).

## 4. Standardized Reporting Figure Layout & Guidelines

When embedding these placeholders into quarterly progress reports, slide decks, or peer-reviewed literature, adhere to the following specifications:

1. **Resolution:** Minimum 300 DPI for print deliverables; 1920x1080 (16:9) for digital presentations.
2. **Color Ramps:** Colorblind-safe palettes (Viridis or standard NOAA thermal gradient: Blue -> Green -> Yellow -> Orange -> Red -> Purple).
3. **Attribution:** Always cite `Source: UTRGV Agroecology / TTFS Cool Schools GIS Modeling Engine (2026)`.
4. **Data Caveat:** Projections assume standard regional irrigation and scheduled seasonal maintenance per the Project Cool Schools SOP v0.6.
