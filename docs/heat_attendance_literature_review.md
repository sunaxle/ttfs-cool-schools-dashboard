# Extreme Urban Heat and Student Attendance: A Literature Review
**Context:** Rio Grande Valley, South Texas, and Donna ISD

## 1. Introduction
Extreme urban heat is an escalating climate challenge that disproportionately impacts educational outcomes in vulnerable regions. In South Texas, particularly the Rio Grande Valley (RGV), the combination of prolonged high temperatures, humidity, and socioeconomic barriers creates significant hurdles for student attendance and academic performance. This review synthesizes current academic findings on the intersection of heat, school infrastructure, and student outcomes.

## 2. Academic Performance and Cognitive Impact
Research consistently demonstrates a direct link between elevated temperatures and cognitive decline in students.
- **Statistical Finding:** Studies indicate that for every 1°F increase in the average school-year temperature, there is a corresponding decline in learning of approximately 1%.
- **Subject-Specific Effects:** The cognitive burden of heat is most pronounced in complex subjects such as mathematics.
- **Cumulative Exposure:** The negative impact is cumulative. Students who experience consistently warmer school years show lower baseline academic achievement compared to peers in cooler environments.

> "Heat makes it harder for students to learn, leading to reduced concentration, increased behavioral problems, and lower test scores."

## 3. Attendance, Health, and Absenteeism
Unbearable temperatures directly contribute to chronic absenteeism, a major focus for districts like Donna ISD.
- **Health Risks:** Students are highly susceptible to heat exhaustion and dehydration. When classroom or transit temperatures become hazardous, parents frequently keep children home.
- **Extracurricular Disruptions:** In the RGV, high temperatures necessitate "no-fly zones" for outdoor activities, forcing schools to move physical education and band practices indoors or to cooler hours to prevent heat stroke.
- **State Policy Context (TEA):** The Texas Education Agency (TEA) provides mechanisms for districts to manage heat-related attendance drops. Under TEC §25.081, districts can apply for a "Low Attendance Day" waiver if attendance falls at least 10 percentage points below the prior year's average due to health, safety, or weather issues.

## 4. The "Cooling Divide" and Colonia Vulnerability
The impacts of extreme heat are not felt equally. The concept of "thermal inequality" is starkly evident in South Texas colonias.
- **Infrastructure Deficits:** Colonias are unincorporated, low-income neighborhoods that often lack basic infrastructure, including adequate drainage, paved roads, and robust tree canopies. This lack of a protective "built environment" exacerbates the urban heat island effect.
- **Home-to-School Transit:** Students from colonias often face unshaded walks or long waits at exposed bus stops, arriving at school already physically depleted by the heat.
- **Facility Inequity:** Schools serving low-income populations are more likely to have aging HVAC systems. The disparity in access to reliable air conditioning is a primary driver of heat-related learning loss, disproportionately affecting Latino and low-income students.

> "Residents often struggle with inadequate cooling options at home, which exacerbates the physical and mental stress of heat waves, making it difficult for families to recover from high temperatures overnight."

## 5. Local Context: Donna ISD and Hidalgo County
Donna ISD and the broader Hidalgo County area are actively navigating these challenges.
- **Historical Climate Data:** The region typically sees average summer highs around 95°F–98°F. However, high humidity frequently pushes the heat index past the 111°F threshold required for NWS Heat Advisories.
- **District Initiatives:** Donna ISD is addressing chronic absenteeism through six-week tracking and attendance committees. Furthermore, recognizing the vital need for climate-resilient infrastructure, the district has promoted career-technical education pathways in HVAC technology.
- **Future Directions:** Simple interventions—such as increasing tree canopies to provide shade (directly aligning with the Texas Trees Foundation's Cool Schools initiative), improving ventilation, and securing equitable funding for facility upgrades—are essential to protecting student health and maintaining educational equity in Donna ISD.
