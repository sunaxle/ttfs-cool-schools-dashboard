### SLIDE 4: Heat and Academic Performance
**Title:** The Cognitive Cost of Warm Classrooms  

* **Academic Research Finding:**
  > *"Without functional air conditioning or thermal mitigation, every 1°F increase in average school-year temperature reduces learning by 1%."*  
  > — **Park, Goodman, Hurwitz, & Smith (2020)**, *American Economic Journal: Economic Policy* (DOI: `10.1257/pol.20180612`)
* **Key Impacts:**
  * Most severe impact observed in complex subjects like **Mathematics** and **Logic**.
  * Effects are **cumulative**—compounding across hot early-semester weeks.
* **Presenter Script:** "Peer-reviewed economic research tracking millions of students proves that heat directly impairs cognitive processing. A 5-degree rise in thermal exposure without adequate cooling reduces learning retention by 5%. Heat physically slows down a student's brain."

### SLIDE 5: Pediatric Health & Acute Illness
**Title:** Heat as a Pediatric Health Crisis  

* **Key Medical Findings:**
  * **170% Increase** in pediatric emergency department visits for heat-related illness in Texas (*Academic Pediatrics*, 2025; DOI: `10.1016/j.acap.2025.102855`).
  * **1 in 5** pediatric heat illness ED presentations require full hospital admission.
  * **The Ozone Cascade:** High heat + humidity catalyzes ground-level ozone (smog), triggering severe pediatric asthma attacks during August and September back-to-school weeks.
* **Presenter Script:** "When it is 108°F outside, heat isn't just uncomfortable—it is a pediatric health trigger. In Texas, we have seen a 170% surge in children ending up in emergency rooms for heat exhaustion. In the Valley, heat and ozone combine to trigger asthma attacks that keep kids in hospital beds instead of school desks."

### SLIDE 6: The Financial Reality — ADA Funding
**Title:** How Absenteeism Impacts Donna ISD's Budget  

* **Texas Funding Rule:** Texas funds public school districts based on **Average Daily Attendance (ADA)**, not total student enrollment.
* **The Daily Rate:** Donna ISD (12,937 students) receives a Basic Allotment of $6,160/student/year = **$34.22 per student, per day**.

```text
┌─────────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┐
│ ADA Drop Scenario       │ Daily Revenue Lost   │ Annual District Loss    │
├─────────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ 1% Drop (129 students)  │ $4,414               │ $794,520                │
│ 2% Drop (259 students)  │ $8,863               │ $1,593,480              │
│ 5% Drop (647 students)  │ $22,138              │ $3,975,720              │
└─────────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┘
```

* **Presenter Script:** "Here is the fiscal reality: Texas pays Donna ISD $34.22 per day for every student in a seat. If extreme heat causes a 5% drop in attendance across our 12,900 students during peak heat months, Donna ISD loses $22,000 every single day—adding up to nearly $4 million a year."

### SLIDE 7: The "Fourth Monday in August" Paradox
**Title:** State Policy vs. District Reality  

* **Texas Education Code §25.0811:** Banned public schools from starting before the 4th Monday in August.
  * *Legislative Intent:* To protect students from extreme August heat and save districts millions in HVAC power grid costs.
* **District of Innovation (DOI) Waiver:**
  * Donna ISD and Mercedes ISD utilize DOI waivers to start school in **mid-August** (e.g., Aug 13) to maximize STAAR instructional days.
* **The Takeaway:**
  * The State of Texas legally acknowledged that August heat is dangerous. Since our district chooses to operate during peak August heat, we have a duty to install protective cooling infrastructure.
* **Presenter Script:** "The State of Texas literally wrote a law prohibiting schools from opening in early August because of heat. But to give our kids more instruction before STAAR tests, Donna ISD uses a DOI waiver to start in mid-August anyway. We are choosing to operate in peak heat—so we must provide the shade infrastructure to make that choice safe."

### SLIDE 8: The Cautionary Tale — Mercedes ISD
**Title:** Case Study: What Happens When ADA Drops  

* **The Incident (September 2025):**
  * Mercedes ISD lost **281 enrolled students** during the first 13 days of the school year.
  * District officials cited "illness" and "inability to locate students" during peak August heat.
* **The Financial Fallout:**
  * Loss of ~$125,000 in state aid in two weeks ($1.73M annualized).
  * **Result:** Forced the Board of Trustees to vote on a mandatory consolidation—merging **Harrell Middle School into Chacon Middle School**.
* **Presenter Script:** "Look at our neighbors in Mercedes ISD. Last August, during the first two weeks of school, they lost 281 students due to illness and absenteeism. That loss of state funding forced their board to close Harrell Middle School and consolidate campuses. Unmitigated ADA drops threaten our school doors."

### SLIDE 9: The Transit & Bus Challenge
**Title:** The Thermal Commute Hazard  

* **School Bus HVAC Limits:**
  * Bus AC units are engineered for a maximum **20°F differential** from outside ambient temperature.
  * On a 108°F afternoon, a bus with AC running at maximum capacity is still **88°F inside**.
  * Backup/spare buses (rolled out when primary units break down) often lack AC entirely.
* **Unshaded Bus Stops:**
  * Students in rural colonias wait at unshaded stops along asphalt roads with zero canopy protection.
* **Presenter Script:** "Even on buses with air conditioning, an outdoor temperature of 108°F means the bus interior is 88°F. And when spare buses run, there is no AC at all. Kids are walking to exposed bus stops and riding in metal boxes, arriving at school heat-depleted."

### SLIDE 12: The Hidden Crisis in Colonias
**Title:** Home Energy Poverty & Night-Time Heat  

* **Colonia Energy Burden:**
  * Residents in South Texas colonias (*Colonia Nueva, South Donna, Scissors, South Alamo*) spend **12.5% to 28% of their income** on electricity (TEPRI Data).
* **Energy-Limiting Behaviors:**
  * Families turn off AC units at night to avoid devastating light bills.
  * Children sleep in 85°F+ indoor heat, suffering chronic sleep deprivation and physical exhaustion before morning.
* **Agricultural Labor Impact:**
  * Parents working "piece-rate" farm labor suffer heat stroke/illness in 110°F heat indices, threatening household income and forcing family displacement.
* **Presenter Script:** "Many of our students live in colonias where electric bills eat up 28% of family income. Parents turn off the AC at night to survive. Those kids aren't sleeping; they are suffering heat fatigue all night. School might be the only air-conditioned place they enter all day."

### SLIDE 13: The Solution — Cool Schools Infrastructure
**Title:** Tree Canopies as Multi-Benefit Infrastructure  

* **What Cool Schools Delivers:**
  1. **Direct Thermal Relief:** Drops campus surface heat by up to 45°F around playgrounds, drop-off zones, and bus loops.
  2. **Ozone & Air Filtration:** Trees capture particulate matter and reduce ground-level ozone, directly preventing pediatric asthma attacks.
  3. **ADA Revenue Protection:** Keeps students healthy and present during August/September heat waves.
  4. **Fiscal ROI:** Planting 15,739 trees (~$2.4M–$4.7M) pays for itself in preserved ADA funding ($3.97M/year) within **1 to 2 years**.
* **Presenter Script:** "The Cool Schools program isn't about planting decorative trees. It is about building living infrastructure. For a fraction of the cost of building new HVAC facilities, tree canopies cool our bus loops, filter asthma-causing ozone, and protect the ADA funding that keeps our teachers employed."

### SLIDE 14: Campus Implementation Plan
**Title:** Targeted Interventions for Donna ISD  

* **Priority Planting Zones:**
  * **Bus Drop-Off Loops & Parent Pick-Up Zones:** Eliminating heat islands where students wait.
  * **Playgrounds & Outdoor PE Spaces:** Creating shaded refuges for active outdoor time.
  * **Pedestrian Commute Corridors:** Shading sidewalks connecting surrounding neighborhoods/colonias to campuses.
* **Species Selection:** Native, drought-resistant South Texas species (Texas Ebony, Retama, Anacua) requiring minimal water once established.
* **Presenter Script:** "We are targeting the exact hot spots on our campuses: bus loops, pick-up zones, and playgrounds. By using native South Texas trees like Texas Ebony, we create shade that thrives in our climate without draining our municipal water supply."
