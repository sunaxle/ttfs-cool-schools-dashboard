# Citation Verification Report

This report verifies three statistics related to heat impacts, learning, and energy burden for the UTRGV research presentation.

## 1. Learning Loss due to Heat
**Claim:** "For every 1°F increase in average school-year temperature, learning decreases ~1%" 
**Outcome:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY VERIFIED

**Details:** 
The statistic and author list are correct, but the user-provided journal was incorrect. The authors found that without air conditioning, a 1°F hotter school year reduces that year's learning by 1 percent. However, this exact statistic and analysis was published in the *American Economic Journal: Economic Policy*, not *Nature Human Behaviour*. (The same authors did publish a related paper in *Nature Human Behaviour* in 2020, which may have caused the confusion).

**Citation:** 
Park, R. Jisung, Joshua Goodman, Michael Hurwitz, and Jonathan Smith. 2020. "Heat and Learning." *American Economic Journal: Economic Policy*, 12 (2): 306-339.
**DOI:** 10.1257/pol.20180612

## 2. Pediatric Emergency Department Visits
**Claim:** "170% increase in pediatric ED visits for heat-related illnesses" (UT Southwestern, 2012–2023)
**Outcome:** ✅ VERIFIED

**Details:** 
This statistic accurately reflects a study from researchers at UT Southwestern Medical Center. Analyzing data from Children’s Health campuses in Dallas and Plano from May through September (2012 to 2023), they found a 170% increase in heat-related illness encounters as a proportion of total pediatric ED visits. 

**Citation:**
Merritt, Taylor A., Sanjna Bhatia, Courtney Solomon, and Andrew Yu. 2025. "Heat-Related Illness Trends at a Pediatric Health Care System, 2012 to 2023." *Academic Pediatrics*.
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.acap.2025.102855

## 3. Energy Burden in South Texas
**Claim:** "Low-income households in South Texas spend 12–28% of income on electricity/cooling" (TEPRI/Brookings)
**Outcome:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY VERIFIED

**Details:** 
The numbers align closely with the data reported by the Texas Energy Poverty Research Institute (TEPRI). TEPRI reports that low-income households in Texas spend, on average, **12.5%** of their annual income on home energy costs (compared to just 4.0% for non-low-income households). Additionally, TEPRI cites U.S. Department of Energy figures indicating that some low-income households pay as much as **28%** of their monthly income to cover energy needs. Therefore, the "12–28%" figure is an accurate reflection of the average and maximum energy burdens reported, though it is not a uniform range for all South Texas households.

**Citation:** 
Texas Energy Poverty Research Institute (TEPRI). Data regarding energy burden for low-income households in Texas. 
*Note: This data is widely cited across TEPRI's publications and summaries on the energy affordability crisis in Texas, incorporating U.S. Department of Energy statistics.*
