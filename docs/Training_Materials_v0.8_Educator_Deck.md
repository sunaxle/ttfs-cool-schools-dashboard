### SLIDE 3: The Urban Heat Island (UHI) Effect on Campus Grounds
* **Slide Title:** How Schoolyards Become Thermal Sponges
* **Visual Diagram:**
```text
  SOLAR RADIATION (1,000 W/m²)
       │      │      │
       ▼      ▼      ▼
  ┌─────────────────────────┐               ┌───────────────────────────┐
  │   UNSHADED BLACKTOP     │               │   NATIVE TREE CANOPY      │
  │  • Albedo: 0.05 – 0.10  │               │  • Albedo: 0.20 – 0.25    │
  │  • Absorbs 90%+ Heat    │               │  • Evapotranspiration     │
  │  • Re-radiates Thermal  │               │  • Leaf Transpiration     │
  │    Longwave Energy      │               │  • Deep Shading Blanket   │
  │                         │               │                           │
  │ Surface Temp: 160°F+    │               │ Surface Temp: 98°F–105°F  │
  └─────────────────────────┘               └───────────────────────────┘
               ▲                                          │
               └────────── 45°F–60°F THERMAL GAP ─────────┘
```
* **Key Bullet Points:**
  * **Impervious Surface Ratio:** School bus loops, parent pickup lanes, asphalt basketball courts, and synthetic rubber play surfaces absorb over 90% of incident solar radiation.
  * **Radiative Heat Transfer:** The blacktop stores thermal energy and re-radiates it upward, baking students from below even after the sun angles drop.
  * **The Tree Cooling Mechanism:** Leaves intercept solar radiation (shading) and pump gallons of water vapor into the air through microscopic stomata (evapotranspirational cooling), physically lowering surrounding air temperatures.
* **Presenter Verbatim Script:**
  > *"Why does the playground feel like an oven? It comes down to basic physics. Dark, dense materials like asphalt and poured-in-place rubber turf have very low albedo—they reflect almost nothing and absorb nearly all solar energy. On an August afternoon, our satellite sensors and field infrared thermometers measure asphalt temperatures topping 165°F. That is hot enough to cause contact burns in under five seconds. In contrast, a mature native tree canopy acts as a solar shield and an evaporative cooler, dropping surface ground temperatures by 45 to 60 degrees. Step under the canopy, and you immediately feel the physics of shade."*

* **Key Bullet Points:**
  * **170% Surge in Texas Pediatric Heat Admissions:** Medical research published in *Academic Pediatrics* (2025) documents a 170% increase in emergency department presentations for children suffering heat exhaustion and heat cramps.
  * **Children Are Not Small Adults:** Children produce more metabolic heat per pound than adults, have lower sweat rates, and their core body temperature rises much faster when playing outdoors.
  * **The Ozone-Asthma Cascade:** Stagnant high heat and intense UV rays catalyze nitrogen oxides and VOCs into ground-level ozone ($O_3$), inflaming bronchial airways and driving peak asthma absenteeism during early fall.
* **Presenter Verbatim Script:**
  > *"When our students play in unshaded heat, it isn't just a matter of discomfort—it is a pediatric health hazard. Children heat up significantly faster than adults because of their body mass ratio, and their sweat glands are less efficient. Furthermore, high solar radiation in the presence of vehicle emissions from bus pickup loops creates ground-level ozone smog. That ozone directly irritates pediatric lungs, triggering severe asthma attacks that land our students in the clinic or the emergency room instead of the classroom."*

### SLIDE 8: Why Native South Texas Species?
* **Slide Title:** Evolutionary Champions: Surviving Drought, Clay, & Heat
* **Visual:** Illustration comparing native South Texas deep taproot architecture versus shallow non-native roots during drought.
* **Key Bullet Points:**
  * **Adapted to Rio Grande Valley Vertisol Clays:** Native species thrive in dense, heavy clay-loam soils that crack during summer drought and waterlog during tropical deluges.
  * **Zero Municipal Irrigation Once Established:** Deep anchoring root systems tap into deep subsoil moisture tables, requiring supplemental water only during the initial 12–24 month establishment window.
  * **Extreme Heat & Wind Resilience:** Engineered by thousands of years of evolution to withstand 110°F+ ambient heatwaves, severe subtropical freezes, and hurricane-force winds.
  * **Maximized Wildlife Biodiversity:** Native trees host 20× more native caterpillars, pollinators, and songbird species than ornamental non-native exotics.
* **Presenter Verbatim Script:**
  > *"Why are we specifically planting native South Texas trees rather than fast-growing non-native ornamentals? Because the Rio Grande Valley has a unique, demanding climate. Our soils are dense vertisol clays that shrink and swell, our summers are scorching, and our winters occasionally bring hard freezes. Non-native trees frequently drop branches or die during droughts. Our native species—Montezuma Cypress, Texas Ebony, Anacua, and Live Oak—have spent millennia evolving survival mechanisms. Once their root systems establish, they need zero municipal tap water and provide unmatched shade for decades."*

### SLIDE 9: Montezuma Cypress (*Taxodium mucronatum* / *Ahuehuete*)
* **Slide Title:** Montezuma Cypress: The Riparian Giant & Living Legacy
* **Visual:** 3D Botanical rendering of *Taxodium mucronatum* showing its massive fluted basal trunk flare, weeping pendulous feathery foliage, and wide crown without aerial cypress knees.
* **Botanical & Ecological Profile:**
  * **Scientific Name:** *Taxodium mucronatum* (Nahuatl: *Ahuehuete* — "Old Man of the Water").
  * **Morphology:** Massive basal trunk fluting (1.8×–2.5× DBH), broad spreading umbrella crown with soft, feathery distichous needle spray cards.
  * **Cypress Knees:** **Absent** in landscape soils (safe for school playground turf and walkways).
  * **Growth Dynamics:** Rapid growth rate of **2.8 feet per year** in height and **0.75 inches per year** in trunk caliper.
  * **Cooling & Carbon Capacity:** Massive leaf surface area generates intense evaporative cooling, dropping ground temps by up to **48°F**.
  * **Cultural Significance:** National tree of Mexico; individual specimens like the *Árbol del Tule* have lived for over 2,000 years.
* **Presenter Verbatim Script:**
  > *"Our first flagship species is the Montezuma Cypress, or Ahuehuete. In nature, it lines the Rio Grande riverbanks, but in our schoolyards, it thrives in irrigated basins and lawn soils. Unlike Bald Cypress, Montezuma Cypress does not form hazardous above-ground knees in landscape grass, making it completely safe for playground run zones. It grows remarkably fast—nearly three feet per year—with soft, weeping feathery foliage that creates an expansive shade umbrella. It is a tree that your students will plant in 4th grade and return to visit with their own grandchildren decades from now."*

### SLIDE 10: Texas Ebony (*Ebenopsis ebano*)
* **Slide Title:** Texas Ebony: The Ironwood Shade Vault & Wildlife Castle
* **Visual:** High-resolution botanical photo of *Ebenopsis ebano* exhibiting dark glossy bipinnate evergreen leaves, fragrant cream-colored puffball blossoms, and thick dark woody seed pods.
* **Botanical & Ecological Profile:**
  * **Scientific Name:** *Ebenopsis ebano* (Family: Fabaceae / Legume).
  * **Morphology:** Dense, compact evergreen canopy, zig-zag flexuous branches with protective stipular spines, rich dark green leaflets.
  * **Microclimate Performance:** The single densest shade canopy of any native Valley tree—casts a **100% opaque shade shadow** that blocks 98% of direct solar radiation.
  * **Nitrogen Fixation:** Symbiotic root nodules fix atmospheric nitrogen directly into the soil, enriching schoolyard turf naturally without chemical fertilizers.
  * **Wildlife & Habitat:** Essential nesting cover for the Great Kiskadee and Green Jay; larval host plant for Coyote Cloudywing butterflies; seeds were historically roasted as a coffee substitute.
* **Presenter Verbatim Script:**
  > *"Next is Texas Ebony, one of South Texas's greatest botanical treasures. If you want true, impenetrable shade where sunlight cannot pierce through, Texas Ebony is the gold standard. Its foliage is evergreen, deep green, and incredibly dense. Because it is a legume, its roots actually fertilize the surrounding soil by fixing nitrogen. Birds flock to Texas Ebony because its dense, thorny branch structure provides impenetrable protection from predators. When planted along playground perimeters or parent pickup lines, it creates a permanent thermal barrier."*

  * **Scientific Name:** *Ehretia anacua* (Spanish: *Anacua* / Vernacular: *Knockaway*).

  * **Air Quality Filtering:** The microscopic mineralized hairs on the leaves physically trap particulate matter ($PM_{2.5}$ and $PM_{10}$) and vehicle exhaust dust along campus bus loops.
  * **Phenology & Wildlife:** Produces profuse clusters of sweet-scented white blossoms in spring/summer, followed by edible yellow-orange berries loved by Plain Chachalacas and northern mockingbirds.
  * **Growth Rate:** Moderate (1.8–2.2 ft/year); forms an upright, compact crown ideal for tight schoolyard footprints.
* **Presenter Verbatim Script:**
  > *"Meet the Anacua, often called the 'Sandpaper Tree.' Students love this tree because the leaves feel exactly like coarse sandpaper when you touch them! But those rough leaves serve a vital technological function: they act as microscopic air filters, grabbing dust, brake particulate, and bus exhaust soot right out of the air before it enters student lungs. In spring, Anacua explodes with fragrant white flowers that attract hundreds of native bees and butterflies, followed by orange berries that bring South Texas birds right outside your classroom window."*

### SLIDE 17: Step-by-Step Lab Execution: Stations 1 & 2
* **Slide Title:** Collecting Thermal & Trunk Data Outdoors (20 Minutes)
* **Visual:** Visual walkthrough of Station 1 (IR Surface Thermometer Scan) and Station 2 (Trunk Caliper Measurement).
* **Station 1: The 4-Zone Surface Temperature Matrix:**
  * **Zone A (Sunny Asphalt):** Aim IR thermometer at parking lot/court blacktop $\to$ Record Surface Temp (e.g., $162^\circ\text{F}$).
  * **Zone B (Sunny Synthetic Turf / Rubber):** Aim at playground poured rubber $\to$ Record Surface Temp (e.g., $154^\circ\text{F}$).
  * **Zone C (Sunny Open Grass):** Aim at unshaded turf $\to$ Record Surface Temp (e.g., $118^\circ\text{F}$).
  * **Zone D (Shaded Soil Under Tree):** Aim under mature native canopy $\to$ Record Surface Temp (e.g., $96^\circ\text{F}$).
* **Station 2: Tree Sizing & Tactile Soil Moisture:**
  * Caliper Tech wraps measuring tape around trunk at 4.5 ft (chest height) $\to$ Record DBH in inches.
  * Canopy Pacer takes giant heel-to-toe steps from trunk to outer drip-line $\to$ Record paces.
  * Soil Moisture Check: Squeeze soil surface $\to$ Check box (*Dry cracker / Damp sponge / Muddy puddle*).
* **Presenter Verbatim Script:**
  > *"During the 20-minute outdoor rotation, teams visit 4 distinct surface zones. The Thermal Scout squeezes the trigger for two seconds, waits for the beep, and reads the temperature aloud to the Field Scribe. ### SLIDE 18: Step-by-Step Lab Execution: Stations 3 & 4

Finally, students quietly observe the tree for two minutes, tallying pollinators and birds. This teaches observational biology while keeping noise levels low."*

### SLIDE 19: Classroom Data Synthesis & Thermal Math Delta
* **Slide Title:** Calculating the Cooling Benefit & STAAR Reflection (15 Minutes)
* **Visual:** Sample student worksheet math box completed with teacher example numbers.
* **The Cooling Delta Math Formula:**
  $$\text{Cooling Benefit (°F)} = \text{Sunny Blacktop Temp (°F)} - \text{Shaded Tree Ground Temp (°F)}$$
  $$\text{Example: } 162^\circ\text{F} - 96^\circ\text{F} = \mathbf{66^\circ\text{F}\text{ Cooling Benefit!}}$$
* **Guided Classroom Discussion Questions:**
  1. *Which surface stored the most heat energy today? Why did the dark asphalt get so much hotter than the grass?*
  2. *How does the tree canopy create a 60-degree temperature drop without using any electricity?*
  3. *If our school planted 10 more Texas Ebony trees along the bus loop, how would that protect students waiting after school?*
* **Presenter Verbatim Script:**
  > *"When teams return inside, spend the final 15 minutes on math synthesis. Students calculate the Cooling Delta: Sunny Blacktop minus Tree Shade. When a 4th grader subtracts 96 from 162 and discovers that the tree produces a 66-degree cooling benefit, the science clicks instantly. They aren't just memorizing formulas; they have physically measured that trees are powerful, free air conditioners for their school."*
