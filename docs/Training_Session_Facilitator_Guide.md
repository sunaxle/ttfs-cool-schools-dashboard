### Module 3: Living Infrastructure — Satellite Thermal Evidence & Economic ROI (18:00 – 28:00 | 10 Mins)
* **Slides:** 9 – 14
* **Objectives:** Show satellite thermal imagery (165°F asphalt vs. 105°F canopy shade), explain school bus HVAC limitations (20°F differential), and present the 1–2 year fiscal payback model.
* **Facilitator Action:** Project Slide 9 (Bus thermal thermodynamics), Slide 11 (Satellite thermal gradient), and Slide 13 (Multi-benefit ROI). Hold up an infrared thermometer to demonstrate surface emissivity.
* **Verbatim Script:**
  > *"Tree canopy is not decorative landscaping; it is high-performance civil infrastructure. School bus AC units are thermodynamically capped at a 20°F differential—meaning when it is 108°F outside, the bus interior is still 88°F. Students arrive at school pre-exhausted.*
  > 
  > *On Slide 11, satellite remote sensing shows that unshaded asphalt in school parking lots hits 165°F—hot enough to cause second-degree contact burns in seconds. But directly underneath a mature tree canopy, that surface temperature drops to 105°F—a 45-degree cooling benefit.*
  > 
  > *American Forests rates Donna with a Tree Equity score of 71, requiring 15,739 new trees. Planting targeted shade in bus drop-off loops and playgrounds costs a fraction of building new brick-and-mortar shade structures, and by preserving just 1% to 2% of your ADA attendance, the canopy infrastructure pays for its entire capital cost within 1 to 2 years."*

## 4. Facilitator Objection Handling & District FAQ Guide

* **Q: "Will this outdoor lab take instructional time away from mandatory STAAR preparation?"**  
  * **Facilitator Response:** *"On the contrary, this lab is 100% aligned with Grade 3–5 Science TEKS §112.14–§112.16 (Thermal Energy Transfer, Environmental Factors, Organisms & Environments). It reinforces tested STAAR math concepts—such as calculating temperature differentials and measuring circumferences—using real physical evidence on their own campus. Teachers report higher engagement and retention compared to standard paper worksheets."*

* **Q: "How do we ensure newly planted trees will survive extreme summer heat and school breaks?"**  
  * **Facilitator Response:** *"First, every selected tree (Texas Ebony, Anacua, Cedar Elm, Live Oak) is native to the Lower Rio Grande Valley, evolved over thousands of years to withstand extreme heat and drought. Second, under our Monitoring SOP v0.6, Texas Trees Foundation and district facilities teams establish scheduled deep-watering bags (Gator Bags) and student Tree Diary monitoring to track soil moisture before heat stress occurs."*

* **Q: "What if teachers or students don't have smartphones or Wi-Fi outside on the playground?"**  
  * **Facilitator Response:** *"The entire student activity is designed analog-first! Students record their temperature readings on paper clipboards. Once back inside the classroom, the teacher inputs the aggregate campus numbers into the portal on a single desktop computer in under 3 minutes."*

* **Q: "How does this data stay compliant with student privacy laws (FERPA / COPPA)?"**  
  * **Facilitator Response:** *"Our system has zero student logins, captures zero student names, and takes zero identifiable photos. All environmental entries are strictly aggregated at the school level (e.g., 'Grade 4 - M. Rivas Primary'). It is 100% COPPA and FERPA compliant by architectural design."*
