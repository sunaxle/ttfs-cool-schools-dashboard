## 1. Session Objective & Key Takeaways

1. **Establish the Urgent Problem:** Demonstrate how extreme Hidalgo County heat indices (111°F+) directly correlate with Average Daily Attendance (ADA) revenue losses and pediatric heat fatigue.
2. **Introduce the Solution:** Position tree canopies not as aesthetic landscaping, but as **high-ROI thermal infrastructure** that pays for itself in 1–2 years through preserved state funding.
3. **Equip Teachers with Turnkey Tools:** Introduce the 45-minute TEKS-aligned outdoor lesson plan and simplified student data logging protocols.

## 3. Anticipated Questions & FAQ Guidance

**Q1: "Is this going to require our teachers to do extra grading or reporting?"**  
*Guidance:* "No. The outdoor lab fits directly into mandatory 3rd–5th grade Science TEKS requirements for thermal energy and environments. Student measurements are aggregated anonymously at the campus level in under 5 minutes."

**Q2: "What about watering and tree survival during summer breaks?"**  
*Guidance:* "All planted species (Texas Ebony, Anacua, Cedar Elm) are drought-hardy native Rio Grande Valley varieties. Furthermore, the Cool Schools SOP v0.6 includes dedicated campus maintenance schedules supported by Texas Trees Foundation."

**Q3: "Where can we see our school's specific temperature data?"**  
*Guidance:* "Every campus has a dedicated portal in the Cool Schools Dashboard (e.g., `donna_rivas.html`, `donna_jw_caceres.html`) showing localized surface temperatures, canopy percentage, and restorative tree counts."
