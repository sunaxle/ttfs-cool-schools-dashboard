Lesson: Birding and Biodiversity

Active Campus: Donna ISD — M. Rivas Primary

Connected lessons: Bioblitz, Trees and the Food Chain, Superheroes to the Rescue, Pesky Pests Matching Game, Nature Journaling

Connected videos: Trees and Ecosystem, Bioblitz

🎯 Objective

Students will observe a tree as a living ecosystem, identifying signs of plant growth, insect activity, and bird life and understanding a tree's role as primary producer.

📖 Overview

Observing nature with curiosity can help students cultivate a sense of stewardship for the land and its ecosystems. As they track their observations, students will practice key skills such as note-taking, research, drawing, and inquiry.

📦 Materials

  • Clipboards
  • Birding and Biodiversity tracking chart
  • Pencils, colored pencils, or crayons
  • Optional: Magnifying glasses
  • Optional: Binoculars

🔬 Method

Step 1: Introduction

Visit a tree on your schoolyard campus (ideally one of the trees that your class has 'adopted' as a part of the Tree Stewardship lesson). Class discussion: What do you think lives in this tree? Connect to vocabulary: habitat, primary producer, observation. Remind students that trees produce their own food from sunlight and that many birds, animals, and insects rely on trees for food and shelter.

Step 2: Observation

Insect Check: How many insects can you find on your tree? If possible: take a photo of the insect and identify using iNaturalist.

Bird Check: Do you see any birds on your tree or nearby? They might be perching, singing, eating or nesting in your tree. Identify the species and count how many. If possible: use Merlin bird ID to identify using sound.

Animal Check: Have you seen any animals near your tree? If possible: identify using iNaturalist.

Step 3: Recording

Create a nature journal with a page dedicated to the nature that was observed today. Have students use the Nature Journaling video as an example. Ask students to draw the birds or insects that they have observed, paying special attention to their size, shape, and color patterns. Once back inside, record all observations on the class chart and into the UTRGV dashboard.

💡 Extension & Helpful Tips

Enhancing your school's habitat for birds with proper nesting sites (like the installed birdhouses or nest boxes), food and water resources will promote bird activity and increase opportunities for observation.

TIP: The Merlin Bird ID app is a fun, free resource that can be helpful in identifying birds! It features photos, bird calls and general information about the bird.

🤠 SOUTH TEXAS BIRDING METAPHOR

"Parts of a Cowboy" Bird Anatomy

An unforgettable way to identify bird field marks on the schoolyard!

🤠
The Cowboy Hat → Crown & Crest

Raised crest feathers showing excitement or making calls (e.g. Great Kiskadee, Cardinal).

🧣
The Silk Bandana → Throat Patch / Gorget

Shimmering throat patch identifying species (e.g. Hummingbirds & Green Jays).

🪢
The Rope / Lasso → Beak Tool Adaptation

Curved seed-cracker, insect probe, or wood chisel (e.g. Chachalaca, Woodpecker).

👢
Boots & Spurs → Talons & Perching Feet

Grasping feet built for locking onto high thorny branches during gusty RGV winds.
