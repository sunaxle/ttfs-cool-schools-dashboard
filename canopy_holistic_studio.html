🌐 5-Tier Holistic Ecological Calculations

Because a school does not exist in isolation, these 5 concentric tiers evaluate the school's impact on safe transit, urban heat, and neighborhood ecological connectivity.

Tier 1 Campus Property (20.34 Acres)
  • TFS Plantable Space: 482,588 sq ft of unpaved turf and open fields.
  • Current Evapotranspirative Cooling: Approx. 3.2°F drop under existing canopies compared to adjacent asphalt.
  • Stormwater Interception: Existing 92,578 sq ft canopy captures an estimated 215,000 gallons/year of rainfall, reducing localized playground pooling.
  • Carbon Storage: ~9.2 metric tons of carbon stored in existing mature biomass.
Tier 2 Outside Perimeter Fence (3,868 Linear Feet)
  • Linear Perimeter: 1,178.9 meters (3,867.6 LF / 0.73 miles).
  • Buffer Function: Acts as a boundary filter against agricultural dust, vehicular exhaust, and street noise.
  • Linear Planting Capacity: Spacing native canopy trees every 25 feet along suitable unpaved perimeter fence lines can host up to ~110 to 140 boundary trees.
  • Edge Microclimate: Trees planted along the southern and western boundary provide critical afternoon shade against intense low-angle solar heat.
Tier 3 Sidewalks & Safe Routes to School (1.2 Miles)
  • Pedestrian Walking Shed: Primary access corridors along Hester Ave and residential feeder streets.
  • Heat Exposure Hazard: Unshaded concrete sidewalks in Donna regularly reach 135°F–145°F during 3:00 PM school dismissals.
  • Target Shade Coverage: A continuous sidewalk tree canopy lowers ambient walking temperatures by up to 15°F–20°F, significantly improving safety for students walking or biking to school.
Tier 4 500-Meter Environmental Analysis Ring (194.1 Acres)
  • 500m Eco-Ring Area: 8,453,947 sq ft (785,398 m² / 194.1 acres).
  • Scale Anchor: M. Rivas campus accounts for 10.48% of the entire 500-meter urban area.
  • Cool Island Effect: Urban forestry research demonstrates that a dense schoolyard canopy can extend thermal cooling 100 to 300 meters downwind into the surrounding community.
  • Runoff Interception at Watershed Scale: Mitigates regional drainage pressure on Hidalgo County irrigation and flood infrastructure.
Tier 5 Surrounding Neighborhood Context & Tree Equity
  • Neighborhood Canopy Deficit: The surrounding residential blocks currently have an estimated tree canopy of only 8%–12%, creating an Urban Heat Island pocket.
  • Stepping-Stone Habitat: Transforming M. Rivas into a 30% canopy microforest provides migratory stopovers for resident South Texas birds and pollinators traversing the lower Rio Grande Valley corridor.
  • Community Resilience: Enhances health and quality of life for families residing within the immediate walking shed.
