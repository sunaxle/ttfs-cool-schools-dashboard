Lesson: Infrared (IR) Surface Temperature & Tree Canopy Heat Relief

Active Campus: Donna ISD — M. Rivas Primary

TEKS Alignment: Science 3.6A (Thermal Energy), 4.6A (Energy Transfer & Conservation), 5.6A (Light & Thermal Energy), 5.9B (Organisms & Environment), MS 6.9A (Thermal Heat Transfer)

Key Concepts: Solar Radiation Absorption, Surface Albedo, Thermal Mass, Evapotranspiration, Microclimate Cooling Delta

🎯 Objective

Students will use non-contact Infrared (IR) Thermometers to measure, compare, and analyze the surface temperatures of various schoolyard materials (asphalt, rubber turf, concrete, bare soil, grass). Students will quantify the cooling relief provided by tree shade and understand how tree canopies mitigate the Urban Heat Island effect.

📦 Materials Needed

  • Non-contact Infrared (IR) Temperature Guns (1 per small group)
  • Chalk (for marking test zones on pavement and soil)
  • Clipboards and printed Field Data Sheets
  • Pencils / Erasers
  • Safety Rules Agreement (Class II Laser safety)

⚠️ IR Thermometer Safety & Protocol Rules

  1. Laser Safety: Never point the IR laser pointer at anyone's eyes, faces, or reflective mirrors. Keep fingers off the trigger when walking.
  2. Measurement Distance: Hold the gun 12 to 18 inches away from the surface (at a 90° perpendicular angle) for the most accurate surface reading.
  3. Paired Sampling: Always measure the *exact same material* in two conditions: ☀️ in Direct Full Sun vs. 🌲 in Deep Tree Shade.

🔬 Method & Investigation Steps

Step 1: Hypothesis & Prediction

Before stepping outside, have student teams rank which schoolyard materials they think will absorb the most heat (Asphalt vs. Rubber vs. Concrete vs. Soil vs. Turf). Predict the temperature difference (Delta) that tree shade will provide.

Step 2: Field Data Collection

Visit designated test stations on your campus. For each material, take three separate IR trigger readings and record the average:

  • Blacktop / Asphalt: Direct sun vs. Tree canopy shaded section.
  • Playground Rubber Matting: Unshaded playground base vs. shaded perimeter.
  • Concrete Walkway: Open sidewalk vs. tree-covered sidewalk.
  • Schoolyard Soil: Bare dirt vs. shaded mulch beneath the drip line.
  • Living Turfgrass: Sunlit lawn vs. grassy area beneath an adopted campus tree.

Step 3: Calculating the Cooling Delta (ΔT)

Apply the microclimate cooling formula: Cooling Relief (°F) = Full Sun Temp (°F) − Shaded Temp (°F). Calculate the percentage of heat reduced by the tree canopy.

Step 4: Class Discussion & Environmental Analysis

Guiding Questions:

  • Why does dark rubber/asphalt get significantly hotter than living grass even in the same sunlight? (Concept: Albedo & Thermal Mass)
  • How does a living tree cool the air beyond just blocking sunlight? (Concept: Evapotranspiration — trees pull moisture from roots and release water vapor through leaves)
  • What would happen to our playground surface temperatures if we plant 20 new native shade trees?

🔬 KEY SCIENCE CONCEPTS
Albedo: Light surfaces reflect heat; dark surfaces absorb it.
Thermal Mass: Rubber and asphalt store heat long after sunset.
Evapotranspiration: Tree leaves sweat water vapor, acting as nature's swamp cooler!
